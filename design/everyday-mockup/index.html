A new page that shows what Doddy does in a normal day.

For someone who has never read a feature list and never will. The page should make them nod: "oh, I'd use that on Tuesday." Three ways to tell that story below. Pick one and we code it.

What stays true on every concept

  • Doddy asks before it changes anything. Every scene ends on a card you tap, never on something that just happened.
  • Everything shown is in early testing, and the page says so. No ratings, counts, logos or quotes.
  • The cards are drawings of the app's confirmation cards, not screenshots. Names and notes are examples.
  • Character art is the owner's numbered pack (the same poses the app uses), not the older SVGs on the front page.

Three things Doddy always does.

Every time.

  • Asks first

    Nothing changes until you tap the card. Quick list edits are the exception, and they can be undone.

  • Keeps your words

    A saved note is your exact sentence, with the date and where it came from. A correction sits on top; the original stays.

  • Stays on your iPhone

    Notes, lists and parking spots live on the phone. Voice, calls and connected accounts leave it only when you switch them on, and the app lists each one.

Why it works

A day is something everyone already understands. No one has to learn what a "tool" or a "memory" is; they watch a Tuesday happen.

The same card shape every time teaches the one thing that matters: Doddy asks, you decide.

What it costs

Eight moments is a long scroll. It needs good copy more than good code, and the moments should be re-read when features change.

Two moments (Gmail, translator) use connected or cloud features and carry fine print.

To build

One static page in site/everyday/ sharing the site's tokens and styles, one small script for the day strip, the cards and the chips. Works without JavaScript (cards show their question, chips hide).

What it costs

·Concept A on a phone

A 390 px screenshot of this mockup's concept A, so you can see how the hero, the sticky day strip and the first moments stack. Taken with headless Chromium; a real iPhone will differ in fonts and spacing.

What changes on a phone

  • The day strip scrolls sideways under your thumb and stays stuck under the navbar.
  • Each moment stacks: time and title, the chat, then the card with Doddy beside it.
  • Chips in "Try saying…" wrap to as many rows as they need; the answer card sits below them.
  • Doddy never sits on top of text. On phones the poses shrink to about 100 px.

·Decisions for the owner

Answer these and the page can be coded in one go. My pick is marked on each.

  1. Which concept?

    A, One ordinary Tuesday. B's door can become one section of A later if people want to browse. C is the most honest-looking but the most work to keep honest.

  2. The navbar label and address.

    "Everyday" at /everyday/, second link from the left. Alternatives: "A day with Doddy" (long for a navbar) or "What Doddy does" (sounds like a feature list).

  3. Phones can't reach it today.

    Add the small Menu button shown in section 0, and the "See a day with Doddy" link in the front-page hero. Either alone works; both is better.

  4. The eight moments.

    Keep all eight, swap any, or cut to six. Candidates to add: "Hey Doddy" from the Dynamic Island, a phone call with memory on, the morning check-in, a Todoist task, a Notion page read aloud. Candidates to cut if you want only on-phone scenes: the Gmail draft (10:05) and the translator (18:50).

  5. Character art.

    The owner's numbered pack on this page (as the app uses), while the front page keeps the v1.3 SVGs until you decide to switch the whole site. Mixing is visible if someone looks closely: the pack Doddy has no backpack or ball.

  6. How honest is "in testing"?

    Every scene shows something built in the app but not yet verified on a real iPhone. The page carries the same "iPhone app, in early testing" pill as the front page and a fine-print line under the hero. Say if you want the label stronger or softer.

What's real behind each scene

  • 7:10 calendar and birthdays: Doddy reads the iPhone calendar and contact birthdays, proposes a reminder.
  • 8:20 leave-by nudge: trip time from the current location to the event's address.
  • 10:05 Gmail: newest emails read, reply drafted, never sent. Needs Gmail connected.
  • 12:40 sums and currency: worked out on the phone; currency uses the ECB daily rate.
  • 15:30 parking spot: kept on the phone, opens in Apple Maps.
  • 17:15 lists: add, read, tick off, undo; shared lists through iCloud.
  • 18:50 translator: face-to-face mode, voice through OpenAI or ElevenLabs while it runs.
  • 21:30 memory: exact words, date and source kept on the phone; correct, export, delete.
  • "Try saying" extras: weather, timers, free time, Wi-Fi note lookup (and "I couldn't find that" when it isn't there).

If we code concept A

  • New files: site/everyday/index.html, site/everyday.css, site/everyday.js. Shares tokens.css and styles.css, loads analytics.js like the blog.
  • Navbar link in site/index.html and in the blog template in tools/build_blog.py; footer links on both; the Menu button for phones.
  • Poses: the twelve pack PNGs from this folder copied to site/assets/poses-pack/, with the provenance line added to the site README.
  • No inline scripts (the site's CSP in vercel.json hashes them). Cards and chips work by keyboard; reduced motion turns off the card tilt.
  • Checks before pushing: 1440, 390 and 320 px, no horizontal scroll, keyboard only, JavaScript off, CSP headers, screenshots into VISUAL_REVIEW/landing/.
