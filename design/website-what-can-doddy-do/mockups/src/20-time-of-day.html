20 · Making the day feel like a day

One quiet device, in the site's own tokens. Three candidates on the same three moments; the paper sun is the pick.

Chosen · The paper sun: the homepage's hero glow travels down the day

What it is. The same radial orange glow that sits behind the hero phone on the homepage, placed behind each scene card: high and bright at 08:45, overhead at 13:00, low and faint by 22:30, gone at 23:30 where the desk lamp in the drawing is the only light. Position and opacity only (two custom properties per moment, no JavaScript); on scroll it is simply where each card is, so there is nothing to animate and nothing to fall back from. One token colour, already on the site, invisible until you notice it, and it works at 390 px because it lives inside each card's own space. The 22:30 scene also moves to the pack's moonlit hills, the one night scene the 16:9 set has.

Rejected A · A handwritten clock in the margin

13:00sticks as you scroll

A Caveat time pinned in the left margin that flips as each moment passes. Cheap and in-token, but it says the time three times (the stamp, the day strip, the margin), it is the site's first sticky element after the nav, and at 390 px there is no margin for it to live in. It explains the day rather than letting you feel it.

Rejected B · The pack's night scenery for the late moments

Honest and pretty where it exists, but the 16:9 set has one night scene (the hills). The pack's night worlds (home, coffee, court, memory, rooftop) are 9:16 portraits that crop badly to a 16:9 card, and darkening a day scene would mean recolouring the owner's art, which the rules forbid. Kept only where it exists: 22:30 uses the hills, as a complement to the paper sun.

Why the sun, in three lines

Already the site's

The hero glow exists on the homepage. Moving it is an extension, not a new element, so the audit has nothing to flag.

Zero cost, zero copy

Two CSS variables per section. No script, no new words, no reduced-motion case, no phone case.

Felt, not read

Nobody will name it. They will reach the end of the page and feel it got late. That is the whole job.
