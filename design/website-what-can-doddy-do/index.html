Review

Recommendation: Direction A, borrowing from C and B

Why A

  • Instant understanding. Headline, Doddy and seven everyday objects are on screen together; nobody has to explore before they get it.
  • It is the same notebook. Taped paper cards, handwritten asides, chapter buttons, pills and panels are all the homepage's own components. The page reads as the next page, not a microsite.
  • Phones first. Visitors arrive from TikTok and Instagram. A vertical story stacks; a map does not.
  • Shareable in pieces. Ten small scenes with lines to screenshot. "The 08:45 one is me."
  • Honesty fits. One label and one caption per moment; the one built feature gets the anchor scene.

What it borrows, and what it costs

  • From C: the object ring is a problem picker, so the impatient can jump straight to a moment.
  • From B: the places become the scene backdrops, so the little world exists without the map's build cost.
  • Cost: a long page (ten moments; six can ship first), a little scroll logic for the current chapter and Doddy's travel, all progressive enhancement over a plain page.
  • Risk: the humour lives in the copy, which needs the owner's ear before build.

Decisions · closed 13 October

The calls, made on the owner's delegation

The owner asked the studio to decide. Each call and its reason; all applied to the mockups above.

  1. Direction A, the tab "What can Doddy do?" second in the nav, route /what-can-doddy-do/, the blog's active rule.
  2. The phone menu, on every page. Without it, Blog and the new page are unreachable on phones except through the footer.
  3. All ten moments ship, in the day's order, with the copy as approved and the 08:45 claim corrected. "Lost in translation" stays as a labelled concept.
  4. The pack is the web art, on the new page and on the homepage (character art A). The five drawn objects stay; the basketball sheet's shooting frame and a pack pose for the court game are build tasks.
  5. Every moment plays in three beats. On phones the card is the page and a "Play the scene" chip plays the beats above it (mockup 17).
  6. The surprise rule is in; no idle blink. Nothing loops; the hero holds still until touched.
  7. Both additions are in: the hero follows the visitor's clock; every moment has a share line. The share image is a crop of the page's own art.
  8. The homepage proposal, all five: one Doddy, the phone menu, the hand-off ("What Doddy does all day" and three moments after How it works), one nav for three pages, the fixed share and search addresses on https://www.doddy.lol/.
  9. Kept for the owner: the homepage's "Voice hasn't been tested on a real iPhone yet" line. The proposal shows the replacement; the site's own rule says only the owner changes it.

Still not built, on purpose: no page under site/, no JavaScript, no deployment. Building is the next milestone and starts when the owner says so. The sources, the renders and the write-up live in the repository at design/website-what-can-doddy-do/.
