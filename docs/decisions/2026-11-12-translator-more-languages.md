# The translator speaks every language ElevenLabs accepts: 52 more, 89 in all

- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decided by:** owner ("Dodaj więcej języków do tłumacza naszego", then "i tak chce więcej języków", then "Okej dodaj make sure it's working" for the live presets, then "Tak rób wszystko" for the prompt line, the tool's codes and a pull request); the first 16 were the agent's pick, the rest is ElevenLabs' own list
- **Status:** code in branch `claude/great-feynman-omk0mn`; **live changes applied 12 Nov 2026**: presets (version `agtvrsn_8101m783vmeae5h8rn6wz8xwjpbx`), one prompt line (`agtvrsn_5501m785efb5fxxrssbrc7ag6hsh`) and `start_translator`'s code lists; what still goes wrong, and the options, are `docs/OWNER_QUEUE.md` item 50
- **Replaces:** nothing. Extends ADR-212 (all European languages) for the translator.

## Why
The translator offered the 37 European languages of ADR-212. The places a Polish owner needs a translator most and has no common language are mostly outside Europe: holidays (Egypt, Thailand, Vietnam, Japan, Georgia, Indonesia), and people working in Poland (India, Vietnam, the Philippines, Bangladesh). The owner then asked for more still, so the translator now has every language ElevenLabs accepts as an agent language.

Read on 12 Nov 2026, the live main agent had only five language presets (de, en, es, fr, pt) besides its own Polish, not the 36 of ADR-212: the European ones had gone too.

## Decision
- **Added (52):** Arabic `ar`, Chinese (Mandarin) `zh`, Japanese `ja`, Korean `ko`, Hindi `hi`, Bengali `bn`, Urdu `ur`, Persian `fa`, Hebrew `he`, Vietnamese `vi`, Thai `th`, Indonesian `id`, Malay `ms`, Filipino `fil`, Swahili `sw`, Georgian `ka`; then Afrikaans, Armenian, Assamese, Asturian, Azerbaijani, Burmese, Cantonese, Gujarati, Hausa, Javanese, Kannada, Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Malayalam, Māori, Marathi, Mongolian, Nepali, Occitan, Odia, Pashto, Punjabi, Sindhi, Somali, Tajik, Tamil, Telugu, Uzbek and Yoruba (`af hy as ast az my yue gu ha jv kn kk ky ml mi mr mn ne oc or ps pa sd so tg ta te uz yo`); then Amharic, Lao, Luganda, Shona, Central Kurdish, Wolof and Zulu (`am lo lg sn ckb wo zu`). 89 languages in all. `fil`, `ast`, `yue` and `ckb` are ElevenLabs' longer codes; `iw`, `in`, `tl` and `cmn` are read as `he`, `id`, `fil` and `zh`.
- **Not added:** ElevenLabs refused Lingala `ln`, Cebuano `ceb` and Chichewa `ny` as presets (12 Nov; its refusal listed the accepted codes, which is where the last seven came from), and Basque and Albanian in October.
- **Their lines:** the AI label, the disclosure, hold-to-talk and the careful line in each, written without a native speaker's review, like the 37 before them (`docs/implementation/TRANSLATOR_LANGUAGE_REVIEW.md`).
- **Telling the two halves apart:** `LanguageGuess` knows 23 scripts now (Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari, Bengali, Thai, Lao, Georgian, Armenian, Ethiopic, Hangul, kana, Han, Myanmar, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Odia, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam). Languages sharing a script are told apart by their own letters and common words: Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Sindhi and Central Kurdish; Mandarin and Cantonese (嘅 唔 係 冇); Bengali and Assamese (র, ৰ); Hindi, Marathi and Nepali; Kazakh, Kyrgyz, Mongolian and Tajik; Indonesian and Malay. A tie is no guess. Latin-script words common in a European language ("po", "na", "di", "ora", "men") are left out, so a Polish owner's line is never learned as another language.
- **Careful mode:** health, law and money words in each. Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Lao and Burmese write no spaces, and Arabic and Hebrew join "the" and "in" to the word, so their words, and two-word ones such as "rumah sakit" and "bác sĩ", are found anywhere in the line (`CarefulTopics.contained`), chosen so they don't sit inside an everyday word (Hebrew חוב is inside רחוב, street; Thai ศาล is also a shrine; "loya" would catch "loyalty").
- **Their half** is laid out from the right for Arabic, Persian, Urdu, Pashto, Sindhi, Central Kurdish and Hebrew.
- **Live agent:** one preset per language in `voice-agent/session.json` besides Polish (88), each with the first message `{{opening_line}}` and the soft timeout "Mhm…" then "Hmm…", like the five that were live; those five were re-sent exactly as read.
- **What the app tells the agent (from the live checks below):** `{{translator_start}}` now opens with "YOU ARE ONLY AN INTERPRETER NOW", with the example the agent got wrong ("where is the nearest pharmacy?" is said in their language, never looked up), says questions and requests are never answered or acted on, that no line is said again in its own language, and what to do after a refused tool. The tool refusal while translating now says the line was for the other person and must be translated at once, instead of "Keep translating".
- **Live tool:** `start_translator`'s `their_language` and `your_language` list all 89 codes, in `voice-agent/tools.json` and on the live tool `tool_6601m4krz5tqe0paj9jxza6vnjgp`; "two-letter code" became "language code (two letters, or three for fil, ast, yue and ckb)".
- **Live prompt:** one line under "Do it first": translator mode wins, no tool but `stop_translator`, and "where is the nearest pharmacy?" is translated, never looked up, answered or followed by "Sprawdzam". Nothing else in the prompt changed.
- **Not in this change:** a separate translator agent (item 50), Settings › Starts in (the ElevenLabs Swift SDK's own list), exact facts in other currencies and number formats.

## Live checks (12 Nov 2026, typed messages over the agent's WebSocket, the way Home › Translate starts)
- **Presets:** a conversation starting in Lingala (no preset) was refused at once (`1008`); conversations starting in Arabic, Hindi, Zulu, Cantonese, Amharic, Italian, Georgian, Thai, Filipino, Swahili, Ukrainian, Lao, Central Kurdish, Shona and Luganda started, translated and sent audio.
- **Translations right** (both ways where tried): Japanese, Korean, Arabic, Zulu, Cantonese, Amharic, Italian, Georgian, Thai, Filipino, Swahili, Ukrainian, Lao, Central Kurdish, Shona, Luganda, Spanish and "detect".
- **Still wrong sometimes:** asked for a place nearby or a price ("where is the nearest pharmacy / ATM?", "how much does this cost?"), the agent looked it up (`find_places`, `look_at_screen`) or asked which town instead of translating (with the old app text, Hindi, Korean and Japanese all did, Japanese in each of 3 tries; with the new app text, Japanese and Korean were translated, Hindi and Wolof were answered, and Spanish got "Już sprawdzam…" before the right translation), said a Polish line again in Polish, and for an English speaker with a Hindi speaker translated into Polish instead of Hindi. After the prompt line went live, ten conversations on each of the last two versions (the first without the line, confirmed from the conversation record) showed the same kinds of mistakes on both: about 3 in 10 place or price questions answered, 2 of 3 English-to-Hindi lines in Polish on both. The line made no measurable difference; the model follows "Do it first" over the translator rule about a third of the time. The options (accept, a separate translator agent, another model) are item 50.
- **Code lists:** in plain conversations the agent calls `start_translator` with `ja`, `zu`, `ka` and (listen) `vi`.
- **Not checked:** speech in either direction (the checks typed; speech recognition in the new languages is untried), the voice's accent, a phone.

## Consequences
- Home › Translate lists 89 languages, and the live agent has a preset for each.
- Asking by voice ("Doddy, tłumacz mi rozmowę z Japończykiem") works: checked with `ja`, `zu`, `ka` and, in listen mode, `vi`.
- An iPhone set to one of the 52 now starts the translator in that language instead of English.
- The lines in the smaller languages (Wolof, Luganda, Shona, Sindhi, Odia, Assamese, Occitan and the like) are the least sure; a native speaker's look matters most there.
- The checks left about 30 short test conversations in the agent's ElevenLabs history.
- No new data leaves the phone: the same audio goes to ElevenLabs as for the other languages.
