Hello, Doddy. Why our favourite first result was 'I couldn't find it'

10 October 2026 · The Doddy team · 4 min read

You knew the parcel locker code this morning. Now you're standing in front of the locker and it's gone. So is the book a friend recommended last week, and the name of the wine you liked at that dinner.

Most of us get by with messages to ourselves, a Notes app full of half sentences, and screenshots we never find again. Doddy is our friendlier attempt. It's a small hand-drawn mate in your iPhone that keeps whatever you ask it to keep and gives you your exact words back later.

This is the first post about building it. It covers what Doddy is, what happened the first time we really talked to it, and what works so far.

Memory first, personality second

The easy version of this app would be a cute character stuck on top of a chatbot. It would be fun for a day. Then one morning it would confidently "remember" a locker code you never gave it, and you'd stop trusting it.

So we started with the boring part. Before any jokes or animation, Doddy had to do one plain thing reliably.

Tell Doddy before you forget. Find the original later.

A few rules come with that. Doddy only saves what you ask it to save, and even in the middle of a conversation nothing is stored until you tap Save. The app says "saved" once the note has been written to the phone and read back, and if that fails your text stays on screen so you can try again.

When you search, you get the note you wrote, the day you wrote it and the words that matched. If nothing matches, Doddy tells you it couldn't find anything. You can edit a note and the original wording stays next to your edit. You can also export everything as JSON or plain text, delete one note or wipe the lot.

There's no account, and notes are stored on your iPhone. Voice works a bit differently. Conversations run through our voice provider, ElevenLabs, and in the current test build your saved notes are sent along when a conversation starts so Doddy has something to work with. The app tells you this up front.

The first real conversation

On 9 October we had the first proper voice conversation with Doddy on an actual iPhone. It lasted 79 seconds. We started in English, switched to Polish partway through, and Doddy switched with us. When we said "Moje urodziny są jutro" ("my birthday is tomorrow"), a Save this note? card popped up. We tapped Save, and Doddy only confirmed once the phone reported the note was stored.

A few minutes later we tried the obvious test. We had saved a code in Polish by saying it out loud, so the note read "Kod: pięć, sześć, siedem, osiem." Then we asked in English, "what code have you saved?"

Doddy searched the phone four times across those conversations, and every time it came back with nothing.

We were oddly happy about that. The note was there and the search simply couldn't see it, because we asked in English, the note was in Polish and the numbers were spelled out as words. Doddy also didn't guess. If it had said "5678" because that sounded about right, the whole idea would fall apart. A mate who makes things up is worse than a notebook.

We also caught a small honesty slip. Before the Save card even appeared, Doddy said "I'm saving this for you", which isn't true if you then tap Don't save. Now it says "Confirm on screen if I should save this" and waits.

What we changed

Search now works across Polish and English, so asking for a "code" finds "kod". Spoken numbers count as digits, which means "pięć, sześć, siedem, osiem" matches 5678. Word endings and small typos are forgiven too, and in Polish that makes a big difference. We've also told Doddy to write codes, dates and times as digits when it saves them, so the problem shows up less often in the first place.

The search fixes pass our automated tests, including the exact question from that first conversation. We haven't asked it again by voice on the phone yet. That's next.

Doddy is a bet. We think people who already text themselves reminders would rather tell a friendly character, and would come back later to find things. Nobody outside the team has tried it yet, so we honestly don't know.

What's next

  1. Ask the locker code question again, by voice, on the phone, and write down what happens.
  2. Lock down voice before anyone else uses it, and decide how long the voice provider keeps recordings.
  3. Open a small early access group and see whether anyone actually comes back.

We'll write each of these up as it happens, including the parts that go wrong.
