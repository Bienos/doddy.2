The short version

  • Your notes stay on your iPhone. They leave it only for the features listed below, most of which you switch on yourself.
  • A voice conversation sends a lot. Your voice, the conversation and your saved notes (except ones you mark Private) go to the voice service: ElevenLabs by default, or OpenAI if you choose it. ElevenLabs keeps a recording and a transcript.
  • Doddy asks first before it reads your calendar, contacts, photos, email and the like, and you tap a card before it changes or sends anything. Public look-ups (weather, web, news, places) run without asking and send only the words searched for.
  • No ads, no selling data, no tracking. The app has no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising code. The website does count visits (see The website).
  • You can see, export and delete. Settings › Privacy › What leaves this iPhone shows where data goes for your settings right now. Settings lets you export your notes and delete them.

3. What stays on your iPhone

The app has no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising code, and it does not track you across apps or websites. These are kept on your iPhone, in the app's protected storage (iOS file protection), and are included in your iPhone's backups to iCloud or a computer like other app data:

  • Notes and lists, with their earlier wordings if you corrected them. Unfinished notes (typed but not saved) are kept for at most 30 days and are never sent.
  • Photos you add to a note, including ones you take with Doddy's camera. Text in a photo is read on the iPhone. A photo you take is not saved to your photo library and is never sent.
  • Voice memos. Their words are written out on the iPhone; the recording and its words are not sent anywhere.
  • About you answers (name, preferences), saved only after you review them, and What I've learned: facts and routines Doddy proposes on the iPhone from your own words, kept only when you confirm them.
  • Remembered conversations (the last 10, as text), only if "Remember recent conversations" is on, and never for a conversation you mark Private.
  • What Doddy did, an activity list of the last 30 days (at most 300 entries).
  • Settings, your private-note list, and the parking spot you save (kept until you replace or clear it).
  • Widgets get a small copy of pinned notes, next reminders and lists. Reminder text is hidden by default.
  • iPhone search (Spotlight) indexes your notes only if you turn on "Show notes in iPhone search"; private notes never.
  • Sign-in credentials for voice and for Doddy's server, in the iPhone's Keychain.

Private notes (protected by Face ID) are kept out of voice conversations, Siri, widgets and the copy for phone calls. They are not separately encrypted inside the app.

4. Voice and AI processing

Doddy's voice, and some of what Doddy writes, is produced by AI services run by other companies. This section says which ones, what they get and when.

Who runs the voice

ElevenLabs runs Doddy's voice by default: it turns your speech into text, works out the answer with a large language model, and speaks it. You can choose OpenAI (its Realtime voice) instead in Settings › Voice. Only the service you chose gets the conversation. The app asks you before it first shares your voice with each of them; Settings › Voice › "Ask again before sharing voice" resets that.

The language model behind ElevenLabs: Doddy's configuration on file names Google's Gemini model, so your conversation may also be processed by Google through ElevenLabs. [OWNER: confirm which model the live voice agent uses at release, and that provider's role]

What is sent in a conversation

  • your voice and the conversation;
  • your saved notes, newest first, up to about 6,000 characters (never notes you mark Private);
  • your list names (a list's items only when Doddy reads or changes that list);
  • your personality settings for Doddy;
  • if you switched them on: your last few conversations (up to about 4,000 characters), your About you answers (which can include your name) and the facts and routines you confirmed under What I've learned;
  • in a morning check-in: today's Doddy reminders and lists with things left;
  • the items Doddy uses with your yes: calendar entries, reminders, contact names with the day and month of a birthday (never the year, numbers or email addresses), message drafts, step counts, how many photos you took and when (never the photos), and results from online accounts you connected;
  • the titles and state of tasks you handed over to Doddy;
  • a random ID made for this installation of the app.

This is sent only while a conversation is open, for at most 10 minutes per conversation. The microphone is used only while you talk to Doddy, or from the moment you open Doddy if you turned that on in Settings.

What the voice services keep

  • ElevenLabs keeps a recording and a transcript of each conversation it runs. At the moment, Doddy's ElevenLabs settings keep them with no time limit. [OWNER: retention period for recordings and transcripts at release]
  • OpenAI: how long OpenAI keeps the audio and transcript has not been verified. [OWNER: OpenAI retention for Realtime voice]
  • A Private conversation means nothing of it is kept on your iPhone. The voice service still runs it and keeps its own copy as above.
  • Deleting things in the app does not delete what a voice service already holds. [OWNER: how users can ask for their recordings and transcripts to be deleted]

Translator mode

The other person's voice also goes to the voice service, with everything said while translating. Opened from Translate on Home, your notes, conversations and profile are not sent. Started inside a conversation, what Doddy already had there stays there, and Doddy is told never to repeat it to the other person. Nothing of it is kept on your iPhone unless you tap Copy or Save within 5 minutes of the end. Please tell the other person that their voice is processed.

Look-ups that run without asking

  • Weather and Wikipedia (with ElevenLabs as the voice): ElevenLabs makes the request for Doddy. A place name and its coordinates go to Open-Meteo; search words go to Wikipedia. The app doesn't contact them itself. These don't work with the OpenAI voice.
  • Web, news and place searches: your search words go to Doddy's server, which looks them up through Monid, a paid search service, and the search providers it uses (Search1API for web and news, LiteScrape for places). You don't sign in to any of them, and Doddy's server doesn't keep the search words.
  • Sums, unit and currency conversions are worked out on your iPhone. For currencies, the app downloads the European Central Bank's public daily rates file (at most every six hours); nothing about you is sent with it.

Claude (Anthropic) on Doddy's server

When you ask Doddy to write, summarise, explain or plan something at length, or to make a document (a presentation, Word, Excel or PDF file, or a long text such as a blog post), Doddy asks you first. With your yes, Doddy's server sends your request and the material for it (up to about 6,000 characters) to Anthropic's Claude. Doddy's server does not store the request; a document it makes is kept on Doddy's server for 7 days so you can open it from a link, and the working copy at Anthropic is deleted after download where Anthropic allows it.

Tasks you hand over to Doddy

This feature needs Doddy's messaging server and is switched off there at the moment. When it is on, and you say yes to handing a task over: the words of the task, Doddy's one-line note on it and its language go to Doddy's messaging server, where Anthropic's Claude searches the web and reads pages for it (so search words made from your task go to Anthropic's search provider). Never your saved notes, lists, profile or earlier conversations. The server keeps the task and its result encrypted until the result is saved on your iPhone, then deletes them; a result that is never collected is deleted after 30 days. The notification has no words from the task.

Ask Doddy (typed questions) and Siri

  • Ask Doddy sends typed questions (and, if you allow it, the saved notes that match them) to a separate Doddy gateway, which passes them to OpenAI. It is off until you turn it on, and the gateway isn't set up yet.
  • Siri: if you ask Siri "Ask Doddy what I saved", Doddy searches on your iPhone and hands the matching note to Siri to read aloud. Siri handles what you say and the answer under your Siri settings, which can include Apple's servers. This is on by default; you can turn it off in Settings.

Training, profiling and decisions

  • We don't use your notes or conversations to train AI models. Whether a provider may use them is set by that provider's terms. [OWNER: confirm each provider's training and retention terms: ElevenLabs, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic]
  • Doddy makes no decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects. It proposes; you confirm.
  • There is no profiling for ads and no scoring.

5. Doddy's servers

Voice sign-in

To use voice in the released app, you sign in once with Apple. Doddy's small sign-in server (hosted on Vercel, with its records in Upstash) checks Apple's sign-in token and then gives the app short-lived access to the voice service, so the services' keys stay on the server. It keeps a keyed pseudonym of your Apple account ID (not the ID itself), your session records and only hashes of your sign-in tokens. It never receives your voice, transcripts, notes or profile. Its logs hold the kind of request, the result and how long it took, without content or identifiers. Settings › Voice has Sign out of voice and Delete Doddy voice account.

Doddy's server for web search, Claude and online accounts

The app signs itself in to this server (hosted on Render, configured for Frankfurt; its database is currently a hosted Postgres database at Supabase while we test) with a random device ID kept in your iPhone's Keychain. The server turns that ID into an account ID with a keyed hash and doesn't store the device ID. It keeps:

  • for online accounts: a link to your Composio user, and each connection's provider, status, granted permissions and a masked account label;
  • short-lived approvals for each action you said yes to;
  • receipts of what ran (tool, time, status, counts; no titles, subjects, search words or text);
  • usage counters for the daily limits;
  • documents Claude made for you, for 7 days.

Your saved notes are never sent to this server.

Doddy's messaging server (not switched on yet)

Notifications from Doddy's server, texts, phone calls with Doddy, daily news and tasks handed over need a separate messaging server. Current builds of the app have no address for it, so nothing is sent there. When it is switched on, it will hold an anonymous account (no name or email), your iPhone's notification token if you turn notifications on, your phone number if you confirm it for texts or calls, the news topics you pick, and a copy of your notes only if you turn on "Let Doddy use my memories on calls" (encrypted on the server, but not end-to-end: the server can read them to answer a call). It would send through Apple (notifications), Twilio (texts and calls), ElevenLabs (calls), Anthropic or OpenAI and GNews (news). This policy will be updated before it is switched on.

6. Memory and personalisation

  • Doddy remembers only what is saved: notes you save, About you answers you review, and facts or routines you confirm under What I've learned. Notes suggested after a conversation are picked on your iPhone from your own words, and saved only if you say so.
  • The voice service gets your notes at the start of each conversation (see What is sent). The rest goes only if you switch it on.
  • Your personality settings change how Doddy talks, not what is collected.
  • When you delete all memories, the app reads its storage back and tells you if something could not be deleted.

7. Connected services and Apple features

Online accounts

You can connect Google (Gmail, Calendar, Drive), Microsoft Outlook, Notion and Todoist. The connection runs through Composio: you sign in with that service, Composio holds the sign-in tokens, and Doddy never sees your password. When you connect an account, its tools are switched on; you can switch each one off.

  • What goes there: only what you ask for, after your yes, such as a date, a search word, or the text of an email, reply or task you tap to confirm. An email you send or forward goes on to its recipients.
  • What comes back: the results (events, email previews, file names, page titles, tasks; one whole email or page if you turned on Read an email or Read a Notion page). They go to the app and, during a conversation, to the voice service.
  • Permissions asked for: for Gmail, reading, drafting, sending and organising email (labels, archive, Trash), but never deleting email for good; for Google Calendar, events; for Outlook, reading and writing mail and calendars. Changing an Outlook event notifies its guests, and the card says so.
  • Disconnect (Settings › Online accounts) deletes the connection record and asks Composio to revoke access and delete it. You can also remove access in your Google, Microsoft, Notion or Todoist account.

Settings › Connections › Pause all connections stops Doddy using your calendar, reminders, contacts and online accounts in conversations.

12. Exporting your data

  • Notes and lists: Export as JSON, text or Markdown (JSON can be restored).

15. How data is protected

What the code does today:

  • Data on the iPhone uses iOS file protection; credentials are in the Keychain. You can add a Face ID lock and private notes.
  • Backup files are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your passphrase.
  • The app talks to Doddy's servers only over HTTPS. Service keys stay on the servers, not in the app.
  • Doddy's servers keep sign-in tokens only as hashes, check every action against your approval, apply daily limits, and keep logs without content.
  • The website sends security headers (a content security policy, and turns off camera, microphone and location access).

No system is perfectly secure, and Doddy has not yet had an independent security review. We can't promise that data will never be lost or exposed. If a breach affects you, we will tell you as the law requires.
